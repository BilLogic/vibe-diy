# Competitive Landscape

Analysis of workshop/event landing pages for design reference.

## 1. Config (Stripe's Developer Conference)

**What works:**
- Clean typographic hierarchy — title, date, location immediately visible
- Strong CTA above the fold
- Speaker grid with photos and short bios
- Schedule with expandable details

**What doesn't:**
- Dense agenda can overwhelm first-time visitors
- Too many CTAs competing for attention

**Patterns to adopt:** Typographic hero, speaker grid layout, progressive disclosure for schedule

## 2. Figma Config / Schema

**What works:**
- Bold visual identity that feels specific (not generic)
- Clear value prop in the subtitle
- Social proof (attendee count, company logos)
- Registration flow is 2 clicks maximum

**What doesn't:**
- Heavy media assets slow page load
- Auto-playing video can be distracting

**Patterns to adopt:** Strong subtitle as value prop, social proof section, minimal registration friction

## 3. CMU School of Design Events

**What works:**
- Academic credibility front and center
- Clean, minimal layouts that let content breathe
- Clear logistics (date, time, location, room number)

**What doesn't:**
- Often too sparse — missing the "sell"
- No speaker personality or approachability
- Registration often buried or redirects elsewhere

**Patterns to adopt:** Prominent logistics, academic credibility. Avoid the clinical/impersonal feel.

## 4. Workshop-Specific Landing Pages (General Pattern)

**Common winning patterns:**
- Hero: title + one-sentence hook + CTA
- 3-4 learning outcomes as a scannable list
- Speaker section with photo, credentials, personality
- Format section: what to expect, duration, hands-on vs lecture
- FAQ section using accordion
- Closing CTA that mirrors the hero

**Common mistakes:**
- Too much text before the first CTA
- No clear hierarchy — everything feels equally important
- Missing accessibility basics (contrast, keyboard, alt text)
- Generic stock imagery that doesn't relate to the topic
